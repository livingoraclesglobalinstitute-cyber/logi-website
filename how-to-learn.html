Pedagogical Frameworks

How to Learn: Advanced Curricular Domains

Living Oracles Global Institute (LOGI) utilizes structured behavioral dimensions and modern cognitive frameworks to craft curriculum tracks. Explore the progressive stages of information processing, actionable target verbs, and structural mastery assessments below.

I. Bloom's Taxonomy Cognitive Domains
B1

Knowledge & Remembering

Recalling, retrieving, and recognizing core institutional facts, structures, terminology, and foundational insights.

Sample Verbs

Recall, tell, show, match, list, label, define, cite, name, brainstorm

Sample Activities

Objective tests, specialized worksheets, quick check quizzes, diagram labeling, reference tables

B2

Comprehension

Demonstrating a clear understanding of facts, translating knowledge ideas, and grouping contextual information.

Sample Verbs

Compare, contrast, demonstrate, identify, report, outline, summarize, review, explain, catalog

Sample Activities

Structured outlines, executive summaries, peer reviews, verification tests, identity exercises, comparative diagrams

B3

Application

Executing, implementing, or applying conceptual theories and frameworks to fresh, real-world operational challenges.

Sample Verbs

Develop, organize, use, select, model, choose, construct, translate, experiment, illustrate

Sample Activities

Analytical reports, interactive workflow diagrams, field projects, video case presentations, case studies, field journals

B4

Analysis

Breaking down complex materials into component elements, calculating inner relationships, and structural designs.

Sample Verbs

Analyze, categorize, classify, distinguish, dissect, examine, differentiate, calculate, solve, arrange

Sample Activities

Mathematical modeling, research breakdowns, team debates, solution blueprints, interactive case-study evaluations

B5

Synthesis

Compiling distinct pieces of information together into a coherent, innovative, and original structural framework.

Sample Verbs

Combine, compose, solve, formulate, adapt, develop, create, validate, design

Sample Activities

Academic articles, research essays, original tracking experiments, multimedia compositions, systemic designs, blueprint graphics

B6

Evaluation

Justifying a tactical stance or decision by evaluating internal records, criteria guidelines, and external metrics.

Sample Verbs

Assess, evaluate, determine, measure, select, defend, score, rank, discriminate, judge, justify, conclude, recommend

Sample Activities

Structured peer reviews, critical self-evaluations, scoring matrices, research critiques, formal recommendation position pieces

II. Fink's Taxonomy of Significant Learning

Dr. L. Dee Fink's framework bypasses purely linear scaling to offer an interconnected circular web. It emphasizes relational, emotional, and spiritual transformation alongside conceptual intelligence.

F1

Foundational Knowledge & Application

Understanding core content metrics (ideas, data, theories) and implementing structural critical, creative, or practical thinking skills.

Core Targets

Understand, remember, use, execute, conceptualize, critique, problem-solve

Operational Manifestation

Enables students to understand foundational systems while gaining the immediate capacity to manage complex practical assignments.

F2

Integration & The Human Dimension

Identifying hidden connections across distinct syllabi and discovering insights regarding oneself and professional dynamics with others.

Core Targets

Connect, synthesize, interrelate, self-reflect, empathize, lead, collaborate

Operational Manifestation

Propels the learner to merge academic principles with real life, building robust ethical values, community leadership skills, and mutual understanding.

F3

Caring & Learning How to Learn

Developing new personal interests, commitments, and internal feelings of care, while establishing pathways to become a self-directed lifelong learner.

Core Targets

Value, internalize, care, inquire, self-direct, research, strategize

Operational Manifestation

Changes the educational experience from passive requirements into an ongoing passion, ensuring students know how to locate, track, and process data long after graduation.

III. Webb's Depth of Knowledge (DoK) Levels

Norman Webb's model shifts focus entirely away from the specific action verb used, measuring instead the exact cognitive demand and context needed to completely resolve an active project requirement.

D1

Level 1: Recall & Reproduction

The baseline retrieval of specific details, equations, data definitions, or algorithmic procedures where a single, clear truth exists.

Cognitive Demand

Requires simple recall of information, performing an established structural formula, or executing basic rote behaviors.

Assignment Examples

Stating biblical passages, tracking dates, identifying programmatic syntax, completing vocabulary definitions.

D2

Level 2: Skills & Concepts

Making analytical decisions regarding how to process data points, requiring multi-step mental reasoning structures.

Cognitive Demand

Requires processing or identifying patterns, organizing data elements, comparing concepts, or executing compound tasks.

Assignment Examples

Summarizing long narratives, classifying historical context data, solving complex multi-step financial logic equations.

D3

Level 3: Strategic Thinking

Managing structural problems that demand abstract reasoning, rigorous proof pathways, and deep strategic planning vectors.

Cognitive Demand

Demands logical justification, defending multi-layered approaches, and resolving tasks that feature multiple valid solutions.

Assignment Examples

Critiquing theological perspectives, designing systems, defending research perspectives against alternative arguments.

D4

Level 4: Extended Thinking

High-level synthesis and cognitive investment deployed across an extended timeframe to design original solutions.

Cognitive Demand

Demands complex conceptual synthesis, executing independent research, and merging distinct multidisciplinary methodologies.

Assignment Examples

Authoring extensive institutional research monographs, creating complete software ecosystems, launch-testing curriculum programs.
