Baptism

A Step of Obedience & Faith

Have you been baptized?

Baptism is one of the most beautiful and important steps a believer takes after receiving Jesus Christ as Savior. It is not a ritual that saves you — salvation is by grace through faith alone. But baptism is the first public act of obedience that declares your faith to the world.

This page explains what the Bible teaches about baptism: what it means, why it matters, who should be baptized, and how it should be done. If you've ever wondered whether you should be baptized — or how — this teaching will help you understand God's clear command.

What Is Baptism?

The word "baptism" comes from the Greek word baptizo, which means "to immerse" or "to dip." In the New Testament, baptism was always performed by fully immersing a person in water. It was not sprinkling or pouring — it was a burial in water, symbolizing a spiritual reality.

Baptism is a public declaration of an inward reality. It does not save you, but it visibly identifies you with Jesus Christ — His death, His burial, and His resurrection. It says to the world: "I have died to my old life, and I now live for Christ."

Romans 6:3–4

"Or don't you know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? We were therefore buried with him through baptism into death in order that, just as Christ was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father, we too may live a new life."

The Meaning of Baptism

Baptism is rich with meaning. Every time a person is immersed in water and raised again, several great spiritual truths are proclaimed:

Union with Christ's Death

Going under the water symbolizes being buried with Christ — a picture of dying to the old life of sin.

Union with Christ's Resurrection

Coming up out of the water symbolizes being raised with Christ — a picture of new life in Him.

Cleansing from Sin

Water is a picture of the washing away of sin — not by the water itself, but by the blood of Christ received through faith.

Identification with Christ's Body

Baptism publicly identifies you with Jesus and His church — you are no longer alone, but part of the family of God.

1 Peter 3:21 says, "And this water symbolizes baptism that now saves you also — not the removal of dirt from the body but the pledge of a clear conscience toward God. It saves you by the resurrection of Jesus Christ."

Why Should I Be Baptized?

There are several compelling reasons every believer should be baptized:

  1. Because Jesus commanded it.

    Before ascending to heaven, Jesus gave His disciples this command: "Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). Baptism is not optional for a follower of Christ — it is a direct command from our Lord.

  2. Because Jesus Himself was baptized.

    Though He had no sin, Jesus was baptized by John in the Jordan River to "fulfill all righteousness" (Matthew 3:15). If our sinless Savior was baptized, how much more should we be?

  3. Because the early church practiced it immediately.

    Throughout the book of Acts, we see that new believers were baptized without delay. On the day of Pentecost, three thousand were baptized the same day they believed (Acts 2:41). The Ethiopian official was baptized as soon as he professed faith (Acts 8:36–38).

  4. Because it is the outward sign of an inward reality.

    Baptism does not save you, but it declares that you have been saved. It is the public testimony of your faith — the way you tell the world, "I belong to Jesus Christ."

Who Should Be Baptized?

The biblical pattern is clear: every person who has believed in Jesus Christ for salvation should be baptized.

Acts 2:41

"Those who accepted his message were baptized, and about three thousand were added to their number that day."

Acts 8:12

"But when they believed Philip as he proclaimed the good news of the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, they were baptized, both men and women."

Notice the pattern: believe, then be baptized. Baptism is for believers — those who have already put their faith in Jesus Christ. It is the first step of obedience after salvation.

A Note on Infant Baptism

The New Testament pattern shows baptism following personal faith in Christ. Every example in the book of Acts involves people who heard the gospel, believed, and were then baptized. Infants cannot yet believe or understand the gospel, so most Bible-believing churches dedicate infants to the Lord and baptize them later, once they have personally trusted in Christ.

How Should Baptism Be Done?

The Bible uses a specific method for baptism. Look closely at these verses:

Mark 1:9–10

"At that time Jesus came from Nazareth in Galilee and was baptized by John in the Jordan. Just as Jesus was coming up out of the water, he saw heaven being torn open and the Spirit descending on him like a dove."

Acts 8:38–39

"And he gave orders to stop the chariot. Then both Philip and the eunuch went down into the water and Philip baptized him. When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord suddenly took Philip away."

The pattern is clear: they went down into the water and then came up out of the water. This describes full immersion, which perfectly pictures burial and resurrection — dying with Christ and being raised with Him to new life.

Not the Biblical Pattern

  • Sprinkling a few drops of water
  • Pouring water over the head
  • Baptizing infants who cannot believe
  • Baptizing for salvation rather than obedience

The Biblical Pattern

  • Full immersion in water
  • Following a personal profession of faith
  • Symbolizing death, burial, and resurrection
  • A public act of obedience, not a means of salvation

What About the Baptismal Formula?

The Bible shows baptism administered in two similar ways. In Matthew 28:19, Jesus commanded baptism "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit." In the book of Acts, believers were baptized "in the name of Jesus Christ" (Acts 2:38; 8:16; 10:48; 19:5).

Both were accepted in the early church. Many churches today use the full Trinitarian formula from Matthew 28:19, while others use the phrase "in the name of Jesus." What matters most is the heart of the one being baptized and the faith in Christ that the baptism expresses.

Does Baptism Save You?

This is one of the most important questions about baptism. And the Bible is clear: Baptism does not save you.

Ephesians 2:8–9

"For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith — and this is not from yourselves, it is the gift of God — not by works, so that no one can boast."

Salvation is by grace through faith in Jesus Christ alone. Baptism is a work of obedience — an outward act that follows an inward reality. It is not a step you take to be saved; it is a step you take because you are saved.

This is why the thief on the cross could be saved without baptism (Luke 23:42–43), and why the Bible says the Corinthians were saved before they were baptized (Acts 18:8; 1 Corinthians 1:14–16).

Baptism Is a Response, Not a Requirement

Baptism is the first act of obedience for a new believer — but it is not the cause of salvation. If you have trusted in Christ, you are saved whether or not you have been baptized yet. But every believer should be baptized, because Jesus commanded it.
