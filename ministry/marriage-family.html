Marriage & Family

God's Design for the Home

Marriage and family are God's idea — not ours.

From the very first pages of Scripture, God established marriage and family as the foundation of human life. He designed them for our good and for His glory. They are not human inventions that we can redefine at will — they are divine institutions with a sacred purpose.

This page explores what the Bible teaches about marriage and family — from the Old Testament to the New — showing both God's timeless design and the grace He provides for every home.

God's Design in the Old Testament

Marriage and family are established at the very beginning — in Genesis, the book of beginnings. Before there was any nation, any law, or any temple, God created marriage. That tells us how foundational it is to His plan.

Old Testament

Genesis 1:27–28 (KJV) says, "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them. And God blessed them, and God said unto them, Be fruitful, and multiply, and replenish the earth, and subdue it."

From the very first chapter of the Bible, we see that God made us male and female in His image — and He blessed the union of man and woman with fruitfulness. Marriage is the context for the creation of human life and the foundation of every society.

Genesis 2:24 (KJV) says, "Therefore shall a man leave his father and his mother, and shall cleave unto his wife: and they shall be one flesh."

This verse is the foundational verse on marriage in all of Scripture. It establishes three great truths:

  1. Leaving.

    Marriage begins with a public separation from the family of origin. A man leaves his parents and forms a new household with his wife. This is a decisive, intentional act — establishing a new primary relationship.

  2. Cleaving.

    The Hebrew word for "cleave" means to be glued together, to be joined inseparably. Marriage is not two people living side by side — it is a permanent, covenant union of two lives becoming one.

  3. One flesh.

    This describes the physical, emotional, and spiritual union of husband and wife. It is the deepest human relationship, reflecting the unity that God intends for the marriage covenant.

Notice that God did not create multiple wives for Adam — He created one woman for one man. From the beginning, marriage is defined as a monogamous, lifelong union between one man and one woman. Jesus Himself would later quote this exact verse to reaffirm God's design.

Proverbs 18:22 (KJV) says, "Whoso findeth a wife findeth a good thing, and obtaineth favour of the LORD."
Proverbs 31:10–12 (KJV) says, "Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies. The heart of her husband doth safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil. She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life."

The wisdom literature of the Old Testament — Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, the Song of Solomon — celebrates marriage as one of God's greatest gifts. It is portrayed as a blessing, a source of joy, a place of safety, and a treasure beyond price.

The Family in Old Testament Law

The Old Testament law provided detailed instructions for the family — honoring parents, protecting marriage, caring for children, and providing for the vulnerable. Consider just a few examples:

Exodus 20:12 (KJV)

"Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee."

Deuteronomy 6:6–7 (KJV)

"And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up."

The family was the primary context for spiritual instruction. Parents — especially fathers — were commanded to teach their children God's Word constantly, weaving it into the ordinary moments of daily life. The home was the first classroom, the first church, and the first place where faith was passed from one generation to the next.

Psalm 127:3–5 (KJV) says, "Lo, children are an heritage of the LORD: and the fruit of the womb is his reward. As arrows are in the hand of a mighty man; so are children of the youth. Happy is the man that hath his quiver full of them."

Children are not a burden — they are a gift. They are described here as arrows in the hand of a warrior: shaped, aimed, and released to accomplish God's purposes in the world. The family is God's training ground for the next generation.

Psalm 128:1–4 (KJV) says, "Blessed is every one that feareth the LORD; that walketh in his ways. For thou shalt eat the labour of thine hands: happy shalt thou be, and it shall be well with thee. Thy wife shall be as a fruitful vine by the sides of thine house: thy children like olive plants round about thy table. Behold, that thus shall the man be blessed that feareth the LORD."

The Old Testament paints a beautiful picture of the blessed home — a wife like a fruitful vine, children like olive plants around the table, and a husband walking in the fear of the Lord. This is God's design: a home rooted in Him, full of peace, joy, and fruitfulness.

God's Design in the New Testament

In the New Testament, Jesus and the apostles reaffirm and deepen God's design for marriage and family. Far from abolishing the Old Testament pattern, the New Testament builds on it — revealing even more clearly the sacred meaning and eternal significance of the home.

New Testament

Jesus on Marriage

When the Pharisees tried to test Jesus about divorce, He responded by going straight back to Genesis — to God's original design in creation:

Matthew 19:4–6 (KJV) says, "And he answered and said unto them, Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder."

Notice what Jesus does here. He does not introduce a new teaching about marriage — He reaffirms the original one. He quotes Genesis 1:27 and Genesis 2:24, showing that God's design for marriage has been the same from the beginning. Marriage is a divine joining — a covenant that God Himself establishes. And what God has joined, no human being has the authority to tear apart.

Marriage is not merely a human contract. It is a sacred covenant — a union that God Himself creates and blesses. This is why marriage matters so deeply, and why it must be honored.

Hebrews 13:4 (KJV)

"Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge."

Paul on Marriage and Family

The apostle Paul gave the most detailed New Testament instructions on marriage and family — especially in Ephesians 5 and Colossians 3. His teaching is profound: marriage is a living picture of the relationship between Christ and His church.

Ephesians 5:22–33 (KJV) says, "Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as unto the Lord. For the husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church: and he is the saviour of the body. Therefore as the church is subject unto Christ, so let the wives be to their own husbands in every thing. Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it… So ought men to love their wives as their own bodies. He that loveth his wife loveth himself… This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church. Nevertheless let every one of you in particular so love his wife even as himself; and the wife see that she reverence her husband."

This passage is one of the most profound in all of Scripture. It reveals that marriage is not primarily about us — it is about God. Marriage is a living, breathing picture of the gospel. Every Christian marriage preaches a sermon to the watching world about how Christ loves His church, and how the church responds to Christ in love.

Notice carefully what Paul emphasizes: both husband and wife are called to sacrificial, other-centered love. Wives are called to a respectful, voluntary submission that mirrors the church's response to Christ. Husbands are called to a sacrificial, self-giving love that mirrors Christ's love for the church — a love that gives itself up completely for the beloved.

This is not a call to domination or oppression. It is a call to mutual love, mutual service, and mutual honor — with each partner fulfilling their God-given role for the good of the other and the glory of God.

Colossians 3:18–21 (KJV) says, "Wives, submit yourselves unto your own husbands, as it is fit in the Lord. Husbands, love your wives, and be not bitter against them. Children, obey your parents in all things: for this is well pleasing unto the Lord. Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged."

Paul's pattern here is consistent: each member of the family is called to selfless, Christlike behavior. Wives honor their husbands. Husbands love their wives. Children obey their parents. Fathers nurture and encourage their children. Everyone has a responsibility — and everyone is called to love.

The Home as a Place of Grace

Throughout the New Testament, the home is portrayed not just as a physical place, but as a spiritual community — a place where faith is lived, love is practiced, and grace is experienced. Believers are commanded to show hospitality, to care for one another, and to build each other up in love.

1 Peter 4:8–9 (KJV)

"And above all things have fervent charity among yourselves: for charity shall cover the multitude of sins. Use hospitality one to another without grudging."

1 Timothy 5:8 (KJV)

"But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel."

Providing for your family — physically, emotionally, and spiritually — is not optional for a Christian. It is essential. To neglect your home is to deny the faith. God takes the family so seriously that He calls us to give it our highest earthly priority after Him.

Four Pillars of a Godly Home

Drawing from both Testaments, here are four foundational pillars that every Christian home should be built upon:

Christ at the Center

No home can thrive without Christ at the center. "Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it" (Psalm 127:1 KJV).

Sacrificial Love

Every relationship in the home must be marked by self-giving love. "Husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the church, and gave himself for it" (Ephesians 5:25 KJV).

God's Word as Foundation

A godly home is built on Scripture — taught, lived, and treasured. "And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children" (Deuteronomy 6:7 KJV).

Prayer as Atmosphere

A praying home is a peaceful home. "Except the LORD keep the city, the watchman waketh but in vain" (Psalm 127:1 KJV).

Marriage as a Picture of Christ and the Church

The New Testament reveals something truly profound about marriage: it is designed to be a living picture of the gospel. Every time a husband loves his wife sacrificially, and a wife respects her husband faithfully, the watching world sees a glimpse of how Christ loves His church.

Ephesians 5:32 (KJV) says, "This is a great mystery: but I speak concerning Christ and the church."

This is why marriage matters so much. It is not just about the happiness of two people — it is about the glory of God and the witness of the gospel. When marriage is done God's way, it points people to Jesus.

In the same way, the church is described in the New Testament as the bride of Christ — loved, cherished, and purchased by Him at the cost of His own blood (Ephesians 5:25–27; Revelation 19:7–9). The intimacy, faithfulness, and joy of a godly marriage is a small picture of the eternal love between Christ and His people.

Raising Children in the Lord

The Bible has much to say about parenting. Children are described as a gift, a heritage, and a reward from the Lord (Psalm 127:3). Parents are entrusted with the sacred responsibility of raising them in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

Proverbs 22:6 (KJV) says, "Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."
Ephesians 6:4 (KJV) says, "And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord."

Notice that both the Old and New Testaments emphasize the same truths: parents are to train their children in God's ways, teach them diligently, and nurture them in love — without provoking or discouraging them. Parenting is a work of patient, persistent, loving discipleship.

This does not mean children will always turn out perfectly. Proverbs are general principles, not absolute promises. But when parents faithfully plant the seeds of truth, love, and prayer into their children's lives, they can trust God to water and grow them in His own time.

When Marriage and Family Are Difficult

Every marriage and family faces struggles. Sin, selfishness, misunderstanding, and the pressures of life bring pain and conflict into every home. If you are walking through difficulty in your marriage or family, know this: there is hope in Christ.

The same grace that saves us is the grace that sustains us. God is in the business of healing broken homes, restoring broken relationships, and rebuilding what has been torn down. But it requires humility, repentance, forgiveness, and — often — help from a godly church community and wise counselors.

1 Peter 5:10 (KJV)

"But the God of all grace, who hath called us unto his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after that ye have suffered a while, make you perfect, stablish, strengthen, settle you."

Do not give up. Do not walk away. Run to God, seek wise counsel, and trust that He can restore what seems beyond repair. He specializes in redemption.

A Prayer for Your Marriage & Family

Pray this prayer over your marriage and family:

Pray This Prayer:

"Father, thank You for the gift of marriage and family. Thank You for designing the home as a place of love, growth, and grace. I ask You to be at the center of our home.

Teach us to love one another as You have loved us. Help husbands to love their wives sacrificially, as Christ loved the church. Help wives to respect and honor their husbands. Help children to obey and honor their parents. Help parents to raise their children in the nurture and admonition of the Lord — with patience, gentleness, and joy.

Make our home a place of peace, prayer, and praise. Let Your Word be taught and treasured here. Let love cover a multitude of sins. Let forgiveness flow freely, and let Your Spirit fill every room.

Where there is brokenness, bring healing. Where there is distance, bring closeness. Where there is pain, bring comfort. Make our family a light that points others to You.

We dedicate our home to You. May it always be a place where You are honored, and where Your love is known. Amen."
