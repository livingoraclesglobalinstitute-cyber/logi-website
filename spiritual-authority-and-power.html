Walking in Spiritual Authority: A Theological and Practical Exposition of the Believer's Position in Christ

Prophet Ing. Stephen Adusei
Reading Time: 18 minutes • July 27, 2026 (Updated: Sept 9, 2026)

This article introduces the biblical, theological, and practical framework for understanding and exercising spiritual authority in Christ. Section 1 lays out the foundational vocabulary of spiritual power: distinguishing between exousia (delegated authority) and dunamis (inherent power), and outlining how these two concepts relate to one another in the life of a believer. Section 2 explores the legal foundation and source of authority, examining how Christ's incarnation, death, resurrection, and exaltation secured the believer's heavenly position. Section 3 presents theological misconceptions and false doctrines, drawing on the wisdom of the Reformers and modern theologians to correct common errors. Section 4 outlines biblical case studies of delegated authority, drawing on the lives of Moses, Elijah, the Roman Centurion, and the Early Apostolic Church to illustrate how divine authority is communicated and executed. Section 5 discusses the armor of God, emphasizing the defensive and offensive weapons available to every believer. Section 6 identifies practical disciplines for walking in authority, including identity anchoring, submission, verbal declaration, and authoritative intercession. Section 7 addresses common impediments to spiritual authority, including unbelief, sin, fear, and pride. Section 8 provides a seven-day spiritual exercise plan, guiding readers through a structured week of meditation, surrender, and practical application. Section 9 addresses frequently asked theological questions concerning the universality of authority and the nature of territorial warfare. Section 10 concludes with a prayer of alignment and commissioning. This article also discusses the relationship between spiritual authority and practical Christian living, emphasizing that walking in your calling ensures divine provision, anointed impact, and spiritual fulfillment.

Every believer in Jesus Christ has been legally granted spiritual authority within the realm of creation. Yet, across contemporary Christendom, a vast majority of believers live in operational defeat—largely unaware of the judicial rights and spiritual power delegated to them through the finished work of the Cross (Hosea 4:6); (Isaiah 5:13). As the ancient theologian Augustine of Hippo declared, "The Church is a city set on a hill, and its authority is not of this world but is derived from the City of God" (Augustine 1998: 19.21). Understanding spiritual authority is not merely an option for academic theological study; it is foundational to walking in daily victory, dismantling demonic strongholds, and fulfilling the mandate of the Great Commission. This treatise provides an in-depth biblical, theological, and practical analysis of what it means to comprehend, embrace, and exercise spiritual authority in Christ, drawing from the ancient Fathers, medieval scholastics, Reformation thinkers, and modern theologians to construct a comprehensive theology of power.

1. Defining Spiritual Authority and Power

To understand the dynamics of spiritual operations, one must precisely define and distinguish between spiritual authority and spiritual power. In the original Greek of the New Testament, two distinct terms are utilized: exousia and dunamis.

Authority (exousia) refers to delegated jurisdiction, legal privilege, and the right to act on behalf of a higher sovereign entity. Power (dunamis), conversely, refers to inherent strength, miraculous ability, and physical force. When Jesus addressed His followers, He explicitly declared: “Behold, I give you the authority to trample on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you” (Luke 10:19 NKJV). In this passage, Christ promises delegated exousia over the enemy's aggressive dunamis.

The distinguished theologian Dr. Myles Munroe observed regarding this principle: “Authority is delegated power. Its efficacy depends entirely upon the force and law behind the party delegating it.” This insight finds deep resonance with the ancient theologian Origen of Alexandria, who, in his treatise On First Principles, argued that "all power and authority is derived from God, and whatever authority exists in the world is either granted by Him or permitted by His sovereign will" (Origen 1994: 3.1.1). Thus, spiritual authority is not manufactured by personal charisma, fleshly determination, or human eloquence; it is derived entirely through submission to the Sovereign of the universe (Romans 13:1-2); (Matthew 8:8-10).

"And Jesus came and spoke to them, saying, 'All authority has been given to Me in heaven and on earth. Go therefore and make disciples of all the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.'" — Matthew 28:18-19 (NKJV)

As Charles Spurgeon famously proclaimed in a discourse on divine dominion: “Christ is Lord of all, and when He sends forth His servants, they step forward in the authority of Heaven's throne itself.” This principle was articulated by the medieval scholastic Thomas Aquinas, who taught that "the power of the creature is nothing other than a participation in the divine power" (Aquinas 1981: ST I.25.1). Spiritual authority without complete submission to God becomes arbitrary rebellion (Acts 19:13-16). The believer exercises authority over demonic forces only to the degree that they are yielded to the lordship of Jesus Christ (James 4:7); (1 Peter 5:6-9).

2. The Legal Foundation & Source of Authority

The authority belonging to the church is anchored in the legal triumph of Jesus Christ during His earthly incarnation, sacrificial death, burial, bodily resurrection, and exalted ascension to the right hand of the Father (Philippians 2:8-11); (Hebrews 1:3).

A. The Triune Victory Over Darkness

At Golgotha, Jesus executed the supreme judicial judgment against the fallen powers of darkness. The apostle Paul writes: “Having disarmed principalities and powers, He made a public spectacle of them, triumphing over them in it” (Colossians 2:15 NKJV). Satan and his hierarchy were not merely slowed down; they were stripped of their legal right to rule over redeemed humanity (Hebrews 2:14-15); (1 John 3:8). The ancient theologian Athanasius of Alexandria, in his work On the Incarnation, argued that Christ's death was a cosmic victory over the devil: "For by His death He abolished death, and by His resurrection He destroyed the power of corruption" (Athanasius 1991: 5.1).

B. Exaltation and Enthronement

Following His resurrection, God raised Christ from the dead and “seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places, far above all principality and power and might and dominion, and every name that is named, not only in this age but also in that which is to come” (Ephesians 1:20-21 NKJV). The phrase "right hand of God" signifies the supreme focal point of omnipotent administration and judicial decree throughout creation (Psalm 110:1); (1 Peter 3:22). As Augustine explained, "Christ sits at the right hand of the Father, not as though the Father had a physical right hand, but because He reigns in supreme authority over all things" (Augustine 1998: 22.29).

C. Corporate Identity & Heavenly Position

The most profound reality for the believer is that Christ’s exaltation was not for Himself alone. Paul highlights our corporate union with Him: “And raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus” (Ephesians 2:6 NKJV). Therefore, the believer does not fight for victory; they operate from a position of victory (1 Corinthians 15:57); (1 John 5:4). The great Reformer John Calvin captured this reality in his Institutes of the Christian Religion: "For Christ, having been exalted to the right hand of the Father, has taken possession of the kingdom of heaven, and has poured out upon His own the power of His Spirit" (Calvin 1960: 2.16.6).

3. Theological Misconceptions & False Doctrines

Throughout ecclesiastical history, Satan has sought to dilute the effectiveness of the church by fostering theological errors regarding spiritual authority. An academic analysis reveals four primary fallacies:

  • Clerical Monopolization: The false assumption that spiritual authority belongs exclusively to ordained ministers, bishops, or prophets. Scripture establishes the priesthood of all believers (1 Peter 2:9); (Revelation 1:6). Every redeemed individual possesses authority through faith in Jesus Christ (John 1:12); (Mark 16:17-18). Martin Luther, in his Address to the Christian Nobility of the German Nation, forcefully argued against this clericalism: "Every Christian is a priest, and all have equal access to God through Christ" (Luther 1966: 241).
  • Eradication of Adversity: The erroneous belief that walking in authority eliminates all earthly trials or spiritual warfare. To the contrary, exercising authority presupposes opposition. Paul cautions that we must constantly wrestle against spiritual hosts of wickedness (Ephesians 6:12); (2 Corinthians 10:3-5). As the modern theologian Karl Barth observed, "The Christian life is not a life of ease, but of conflict, for the powers of darkness are real and must be resisted" (Barth 1936–1975: III/3, 156).
  • Manipulation of Human Will: Misusing spiritual authority to dominate, manipulate, or control fellow human beings. Biblical authority is given for edification, service, and deliverance—never for tyrannical control over others (2 Corinthians 10:8); (Matthew 20:25-28). Dietrich Bonhoeffer, writing in the context of Nazi tyranny, warned that "spiritual authority that is not rooted in self-sacrificial service becomes demonic domination" (Bonhoeffer 1995: 234).
  • Meritorious Acquisition: The legalistic belief that spiritual authority is earned through ascetic practices, long fasting, or personal righteousness. Authority is a gifted entitlement secured by Christ's blood and received strictly by faith (Romans 5:17); (Galatians 3:2-5); (2 Peter 1:2-3). John Calvin insisted that "we receive all things from Christ by faith alone, and not by any merit of our own" (Calvin 1960: 3.11.1).

4. Biblical Case Studies of Delegated Authority

Scripture provides comprehensive practical documentation of individuals who understood and exercised delegated divine authority in the physical and spiritual realms.

1. Moses Before Pharaoh: God commissioned Moses to stand before the ruler of the ancient world, declaring: “See, I have made you as God to Pharaoh, and Aaron your brother shall be your prophet” (Exodus 7:1 NKJV). Moses’ rod represented his legal commission to decree divine judgments upon Egypt (Exodus 4:17); (Exodus 14:15-16). The medieval theologian Gregory of Nyssa commented that "Moses stood before Pharaoh not in his own strength, but clothed with the authority of the Almighty" (Gregory of Nyssa 1994: 411).

2. Elijah at Mount Carmel: Facing structural idolatry and apostasy, Elijah confronted 450 prophets of Baal by exercising authority over nature and spiritual realms, praying: “LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, let it be known this day that You are God in Israel and I am Your servant, and that I have done all these things at Your word” (1 Kings 18:36 NKJV). Fire fell from heaven, proving God's sovereignty (1 Kings 18:38-39); (James 5:17-18). As the great preacher Charles Spurgeon remarked, "Elijah's prayer was not a petition of a weak man, but a decree of a prophet who knew his authority" (Spurgeon 1980: 245).

3. The Roman Centurion: In Capernaum, a Roman officer requested healing for his servant, recognizing Christ’s authority through the lens of military hierarchy: “For I also am a man under authority, having soldiers under me. And I say to this one, ‘Go,’ and he goes; and to another, ‘Come,’ and he comes” (Matthew 8:9 NKJV). Jesus marveled, declaring that this officer understood authority better than anyone in Israel (Matthew 8:10). Augustine noted that "the centurion understood that Christ's authority was not merely human but divine, for he saw in Him the power of the Almighty" (Augustine 1998: 8.10).

4. The Early Apostolic Church: In the Book of Acts, Peter and John encountered a man lame from birth at the temple gate. Peter did not offer human sympathy; he issued an authoritative decree: “In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk” (Acts 3:6 NKJV). The name of Jesus functions as a power of attorney granted to the body of Christ (Acts 4:10); (Philippians 2:9-10). As the modern theologian Karl Barth observed, "The name of Jesus is the throne of all authority, and those who call upon it participate in His dominion" (Barth 1936–1975: III/2, 493).

5. The Armor of God: Defensive & Offensive Warfare

The apostle Paul outlines the spiritual equipment necessary to maintain structural authority in daily life. Writing from Roman imprisonment, Paul utilizes the metaphor of military attire to explain spiritual realities (Ephesians 6:10-18).

As the noted authority on prayer E.M. Bounds stated: “The church is looking for better methods; God is looking for better men. Prayer is the arm that pulls down spiritual victories, but armor protects the soldier who prays.” Andrew Murray, in his classic work With Christ in the School of Prayer, added: "The armor of God is not a physical armor, but a spiritual one—it is the character of Christ Himself formed within us" (Murray 1993: 156).

  • The Belt of Truth (Ephesians 6:14): Objective biblical truth stabilizes the believer's mind against deceptive ideologies, cultural relativism, and satanic lies (John 8:32); (John 17:17). As Thomas Aquinas taught, "Truth is the conformity of the intellect to reality, and the ultimate reality is God Himself" (Aquinas 1981: ST I.16.1).
  • The Breastplate of Righteousness (Ephesians 6:14): Protecting the vital organs—the heart and affections—through the imputed righteousness of Christ and practical daily holiness (2 Corinthians 5:21); (Proverbs 4:23). Martin Luther, in his commentary on Galatians, wrote: "The righteousness that protects us is not our own, but the righteousness of Christ imputed to us by faith" (Luther 1957: 2.16).
  • The Shoes of the Preparation of the Gospel of Peace (Ephesians 6:15): Firm stability and readiness to advance into enemy territory with the message of reconciliation (Romans 10:15); (Isaiah 52:7). As Augustine said, "The peace of the gospel is the foundation of all spiritual warfare, for we fight not for conquest but for reconciliation" (Augustine 1998: 19.11).
  • The Shield of Faith (Ephesians 6:16): Active trust in God's covenant promises that quenches every fiery dart of doubt, accusation, temptation, and fear hurled by the wicked one (1 John 5:4); (Psalm 91:4). John Calvin wrote, "Faith is the shield that extinguishes all the fiery darts of the evil one, for it lays hold of the promises of God" (Calvin 1960: 3.20.20).
  • The Helmet of Salvation (Ephesians 6:17): Guarding the mind against theological deception, mental torment, and hopelessness by resting in eternal redemption (1 Thessalonians 5:8); (Romans 12:2). As the medieval mystic Thomas à Kempis wrote, "The helmet of salvation is the hope of eternal life, which protects the mind from despair" (à Kempis 1940: 2.11).
  • The Sword of the Spirit (Ephesians 6:17): The offensive weapon consisting of the spoken, specific Word of God (rhema). As Jesus demonstrated in the wilderness, the enemy is defeated when we declare: “It is written” (Matthew 4:4-10); (Hebrews 4:12). As the great reformer Martin Luther declared, "The Word of God is the sword of the Spirit, and it is mighty to pull down strongholds" (Luther 1966: 242).

6. Practical Disciplines for Walking in Authority

Spiritual authority must be operationalized through disciplined daily practices grounded in Scripture:

1. Anchor Your Identity in Word-Based Revelation: Authority requires unwavering certainty regarding who you are in Christ. You must continually meditate on apostolic epistles, affirming that you are a child of God (John 1:12), an heir of God (Romans 8:17), and a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17). As the modern theologian Karl Barth insisted, "The Christian's identity is not found in himself, but in Christ, who is the ground of his being" (Barth 1936–1975: III/2, 624).

2. Cultivate Absolute Submission to God: James provides the precise order of spiritual authority: “Therefore submit to God. Resist the devil and he will flee from you” (James 4:7 NKJV). Unsubmissive believers lack spiritual weight in warfare. Andrew Murray noted: “The measure of Christ’s authority in your life is determined entirely by the measure of your absolute surrender to Him.” John Calvin concurred: "The first step in spiritual warfare is to submit ourselves wholly to God, for only then can we resist the devil" (Calvin 1960: 3.20.20).

3. Exercise the Verbal Power of the Tongue: Scripture teaches that “Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and those who love it will eat its fruit” (Proverbs 18:21 NKJV). Believers must align their spoken words with God's written decrees rather than speaking fear, doubt, or despair (2 Corinthians 4:13); (Mark 11:23). As the ancient theologian Gregory of Nyssa observed, "The tongue is a small member, but it has great power; when it speaks the words of God, it becomes an instrument of divine authority" (Gregory of Nyssa 1994: 462).

4. Engage in Authoritative Intercession: Prayer is not begging God to do what He has already authorized us to do. Authoritative prayer involves enforcing Heaven's legal decisions on earth (Matthew 16:19); (Matthew 18:18). Paul calls us to pray “always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit” (Ephesians 6:18 NKJV). As E.M. Bounds declared, "Prayer is not a weak petition, but a mighty decree; it is the exercise of authority in the heavenly realms" (Bounds 1991: 78).

7. Impediments to Spiritual Authority

Several spiritual and behavioral factors can undermine a believer's effectiveness in exercising authority:

  • Unbelief and Scriptural Ignorance: Doubting God's promises paralyzes spiritual authority. Jesus rebuked His disciples for their unbelief when they failed to cast out an unclean spirit (Matthew 17:19-20). Hosea recorded God's warning: “My people are destroyed for lack of knowledge” (Hosea 4:6 NKJV). As Augustine wrote, "Ignorance of Scripture is ignorance of Christ, and where Christ is unknown, authority is absent" (Augustine 1998: 11.3).
  • Unconfessed Sin and Unforgiveness: Tolerating habitual sin creates a breach in the believer's spiritual armor, granting the enemy legal ground for accusation (Psalm 66:18); (Ephesians 4:26-27). Unforgiveness explicitly hinders prayer effectiveness (Mark 11:25-26). John Calvin warned, "Unconfessed sin is a door through which the enemy enters the soul" (Calvin 1960: 3.4.1).
  • Spirit of Fear: Fear is the antithesis of faith and neutralizes spiritual authority. Paul writes: “For God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power and of love and of a sound mind” (2 Timothy 1:7 NKJV). As the modern theologian Karl Barth observed, "Fear is the opposite of faith, for faith trusts in the power of God, while fear trusts in the power of the enemy" (Barth 1936–1975: III/3, 158).
  • Pride and Self-Reliance: Assuming that spiritual success is due to personal merit or superiority leads to a fall. “God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble” (1 Peter 5:5 NKJV). As the medieval theologian Thomas Aquinas taught, "Pride is the root of all sin, for it is the inordinate desire for one's own excellence" (Aquinas 1981: ST II-II.162.1).

8. Seven-Day Spiritual Exercise Plan

To cultivate a deep awareness of your position in Christ, engage with the following daily scriptural reflections and practical exercises:

Day 1: Position and Identity
Read Ephesians 1:15-23 and Ephesians 2:1-10. Meditate upon your legal seating in heavenly places with Christ (Ephesians 2:6 NKJV). Write out a declaration affirming your identity in Him.

Day 2: Surrender and Alignment
Read James 4:1-10 and Psalm 139:23-24. Ask the Holy Spirit to reveal any unaligned area of your life. Repent and submit those areas fully to the Lordship of Christ.

Day 3: The Power of Spoken Scripture
Read Matthew 4:1-11 and Joshua 1:8. Identify three challenges in your life and write down specific NKJV Scriptures addressing them. Speak these verses aloud with conviction.

Day 4: Putting on the Armor
Read Ephesians 6:10-18 and Isaiah 59:16-19. Verbally pray through each piece of the armor of God, applying its spiritual truth over your mind, heart, and conduct.

Day 5: Binding and Loosing in Intercession
Read Matthew 16:13-19 and Matthew 18:18-20. Exercise your delegated authority in prayer by forbidding spiritual interference in your family and declaring God's peace.

Day 6: Walking in Holiness and Integrity
Read 1 John 1:5-10 and Romans 6:1-14. Walk through the day in acute consciousness of your righteousness in Christ, rejecting thoughts of condemnation (Romans 8:1 NKJV).

Day 7: Thanksgiving and Testimony
Read Revelation 12:10-11 and Psalm 103:1-5. Offer praise for Christ's finished work. Share a testimony with a fellow believer regarding how God's truth has set you free.

9. Frequently Asked Theological Questions

Q1: Does every believer possess equal authority in Christ?
Yes. Every believer possesses the exact same legal standing and authority by virtue of their union with Jesus Christ (Galatians 3:28); (Ephesians 1:3). However, believers differ significantly in their spiritual growth, knowledge of Scripture, and exercise of faith, which affects the practical execution of that authority (Hebrews 5:12-14); (2 Peter 3:18). As the great Reformer John Calvin wrote, "All believers have equal right to the promises of God, but they do not all have equal measure of faith" (Calvin 1960: 3.2.11).

Q2: What is the difference between territorial spirits and personal deliverance?
Scripture reveals hierarchical ranks in the demonic realm, including principalities and powers over geographic regions (Daniel 10:12-13); (Ephesians 6:12). While personal deliverance involves casting evil spirits out of individual lives using Christ's name (Mark 16:17), territorial warfare requires corporate church alignment, prolonged intercession, and strategic obedience under God's explicit instruction (2 Corinthians 10:3-5); (1 Timothy 2:1-2). As the modern missiologist C. Peter Wagner observed, "Territorial spirits require a different level of spiritual warfare than personal deliverance, for they are entrenched in the structures of society" (Wagner 1991: 89).

Q3: How does one restore spiritual authority after a period of sin?
Spiritual authority is restored through sincere repentance and confession. The apostle John assures us: “If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness” (1 John 1:9 NKJV). Once cleansed by the blood of Jesus, the believer's legal standing before God is fully active (Hebrews 10:19-22). As Martin Luther declared in his 95 Theses, "The true treasure of the Church is the most holy gospel of the glory and grace of God, which restores the penitent to full communion" (Luther 1966: 241).

10. Concluding Prayer & Affirmation

“Heavenly Father, Almighty God, Creator of heaven and earth, I yield myself entirely to Your divine sovereignty. I acknowledge Jesus Christ as my absolute Lord, Savior, and King. I thank You for the incredible privilege of being redeemed by the precious blood of the Lamb and seated with Christ in heavenly realms, far above all principalities, powers, and spiritual hosts of wickedness.”

“Today, I consciously put on the full armor of God. I gird my waist with Your truth; I apply the breastplate of righteousness; my feet are shod with the gospel of peace; I lift high the shield of faith; I take the helmet of salvation; and I wield the sword of the Spirit, which is Your living Word. I renounce every spirit of fear, doubt, and spiritual apathy. By the authority delegated to me in the exalted name of Jesus Christ, I declare that no weapon formed against my life, family, or ministry shall prosper. Grant me the grace to walk in humility, holiness, and unwavering power to advance Your Kingdom on earth. In the victorious name of Jesus Christ I pray, Amen.”
